Plate II About

A budding researcher, trained first on outbreaks, now on arteries.

Hi, I am Siam. I study CD8+ T cells: the cytotoxic soldiers of the immune system. They are supposed to be disciplined. They recognise something, they act, and then most of them die off. As we age, a subset stops following that script: it accumulates, holds onto an activated state, and turns up in tissue where it has no obvious business being, including the fatty lesions that form inside arteries.

My thesis asks what those cells are doing there. Which states do they occupy, how do they arrive, and does their presence make atherosclerosis worse, or is it incidental to it? I work in mouse models and read the immune system out by spectral flow cytometry and single-cell RNA sequencing.

I trained as a microbiologist at the University of Dhaka and worked at the Biomedical Research Foundation, icddr,b and BRAC University before starting graduate school in the US. That work was epidemiological: field data collection during the 2017 chikungunya outbreak in Dhaka, and a run of studies on how COVID-19 moved through Bangladesh.

Plate IV Research

From thirty markers per cell to a named population.

  1. I

    Aging CD8+ T cells

    As an animal ages, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells accumulate and hold onto an activated state. I characterise these age-associated populations and the Granzyme K subset that grows more common with age.

  2. II

    T cells in atherosclerosis

    Age-associated T cells turn up inside atherosclerotic plaques. My thesis asks which states they occupy, how they arrive, and whether their presence drives the disease or simply travels with it.

  3. III

    High-dimensional cytometry

    A spectral panel reads thirty-plus markers on every cell that passes the laser. I design panels and analysis pipelines that keep that information readable instead of collapsing it.

  4. IV

    Computational immunology

    My master's thesis in Dhaka was the first time I had to build an analysis rather than run one. That habit became CAFE, an open-source web app for spectral flow cytometry.

Plate V Software

CAFE

Cell Analyzer for Flow Experiment. Cytometry analysis, no code required.

CAFE is an open-source web app that runs the whole pipeline on a spectral flow cytometry dataset: dimensionality reduction, clustering, differential abundance testing, and publication-ready figures. Most labs assemble that sequence by hand, in a different script every time. CAFE puts it behind an interface a bench immunologist can drive alone. Python, Streamlit and Scanpy, MIT licensed.

Read the paper Source on GitHub

The pipeline CAFE runs

  1. Dimensionality reduction on high-dimensional panels
  2. Clustering into named cell populations
  3. Differential abundance testing across conditions
  4. Visualization and figures out the far end

Siam MHB, Ali MA, Vardaman D III, Acharyya S, Patil M, Tyrrell DJ. Bioinformatics, 2025. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf176
